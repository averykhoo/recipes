# Ratatouille (Bocuse Restaurant Style)

A guess at recreating a ratatouille eaten at the Bocuse restaurant. This version worked, though it's not a perfect match — see notes for the remaining gap.

Serves 2 (small), with a slice or two of sourdough each, toasted but not too crispy.

## Ingredients

### Confit vegetables (made ahead — keeps for months)
* 2 red bell peppers, cleaned and cut into strips, confit in oil (~1-2 hours)
* 3 carrots, peeled and diced, confit in oil (~1 hour)
* Garlic, confit in oil (~2-3 hours) — optional, whole cloves are fine

### Onion gravy
* 0.5 onion, chopped
* Butter
* Stock
* Red wine

### Zucchini
* 2 zucchini, sliced (on the thicker side)

### To finish
* 0.5-1 cup tomato sauce (bottled passata is fine)
* Fresh herbs (e.g. basil)
* Salt, pepper, paprika
* Splash of red wine, for deglazing

## Directions

1. Cook the onion in butter until caramelized.
2. Add stock and cook down until it thickens to a sauce.
3. Add red wine and reduce until it becomes a paste. Set aside.
4. If the confit vegetables are cold, warm them first so the oil loosens, then drain off as much oil as you can — a residual tablespoon or two left behind is fine.
5. Fry the pepper strips and carrot cubes in the residual oil with salt, pepper, and paprika.
6. Add the tomato sauce and cook until it reduces to a dry paste, not a liquid sauce. Stir in the herbs.
7. Deglaze with a splash of red wine and cook it off.
8. Spread the vegetable base into a small shallow dish, about 1 cm high.
9. Top with the onion paste.
10. Bake the zucchini slices dry (no oil) and layer on top, arranged ratatouille-style.
11. Serve with lightly toasted sourdough.

## Notes (gap vs. the Bocuse version)

* Peppers came out too soft this round — try frying separately, or for less time, next time.
* Garlic: contributed well; try smaller pieces next time (whole cloves were also fine).
* Onion unconfirmed — added mainly for sweetness, but peppers may be sweet enough alone. Bocuse's version was probably shallots (sautéed/sweated), not a reduced onion gravy, and likely without stock — traditional ratatouille has none. Could try a splash of beef stock in the fry step instead, if depth is missing.
* At Bocuse: veg was in neat small cubes (not strips), fried harder — caramelized and lightly charred (almost burnt, not quite), no visible pool of sauce around it.
* Zucchini at Bocuse: thin-sliced (mostly yellow, some green), maybe ~1mm, lightly oiled (EVOO), charred just before serving (likely under a salamander), arranged in the movie's spiral pattern. Have a blowtorch — worth trying as a charring substitute next time.
* Sticking with thick zucchini slices on purpose here — easier to cook at home, and more substantial for a main-sized portion.
* Not the same dish as [confit byaldi](./ratatouille-movie-version.md) (NYT/movie recipe) — only the zucchini's spiral arrangement borrows from that.
* Not done this round, but part of the original plate: a peeled cherry tomato stuffed with pesto, plus a single small yellow edible petal, on top.
* Restaurant portion was a small tasting dish; this recipe is scaled up toward a light main.
